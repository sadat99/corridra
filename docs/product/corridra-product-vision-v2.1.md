## 1. What Corridra Is

Corridra is a globally configurable cross-border market-access and commercial mission platform.

It helps companies and institutions turn international commercial intentions into qualified counterparties, trusted interactions, coordinated execution and measurable commercial progress.

Its operating principle:

**Know who matters. Create access. Execute intelligently. Move the opportunity forward. Measure what becomes business.**

Corridra does not exist to provide more company names, automate generic prospecting or create another supplier directory.

It solves the operational gap between:

"We want to do business in this market."

and:

"We have identified relevant counterparties, engaged them, validated important information, executed the necessary actions and know exactly what is progressing commercially."

Public category: **Cross-Border Market Access**
Strategic promise: **Make market entry deliberate.**
Internal category: **Cross-Border Market Access Infrastructure.**

## 2. The Problems Corridra Solves

International commerce is fragmented across:

- Trade fairs and business delegations.
- Business directories and contact databases.
- Chambers, trade agencies and embassies.
- Sourcing agents, distributors and consultants.
- Personal relationships and diaspora networks.
- Emails, WeChat, WhatsApp and spreadsheets.
- Supplier inspections and verification services.
- RFQs, quotations, negotiations and commercial documents.
- CRM systems and post-meeting follow-up.

Companies spend money on market visits, sourcing and internationalisation without a coordinated system connecting intent to commercial outcomes.

Common failures include:

- Targeting the wrong companies.
- Spending on fairs without meaningful meetings.
- Collecting contacts without reaching decision-makers.
- Selecting unsuitable suppliers.
- Incomparable machinery or product quotations.
- Unreliable company/capability claims.
- Poor coordination of factory visits and meetings.
- Commercial opportunities disappearing after meetings.
- Dependence on opaque local intermediaries.
- Lack of measurable return from delegations.
- Little reusable intelligence from previous international activity.

Corridra combines existing data, relationships, evidence and human execution around a specific commercial objective.

It must outperform the customer's existing combination of employees, agents, events, spreadsheets and personal networks, not merely look better than competing software.

## 3. The Global Product Ambition

Corridra must support commercial corridors between any two countries through one reusable foundation.

Examples:

- Bangladesh ↔ China.
- Bangladesh ↔ Spain.
- Bangladesh ↔ Germany.
- China ↔ Spain.
- India ↔ UAE.
- Turkey ↔ Germany.
- Brazil ↔ Mexico.
- Any other legally and operationally supportable combination.

A corridor has directional routes.

Bangladesh → China and China → Bangladesh are different commercial routes, even though they belong to the same corridor.

Markets may also involve trade blocs, subnational regions, cities and industrial clusters.

The EU is a market bloc, not a country. National laws, service coverage and commercial constraints must remain independently addressable.

One organisation may be a buyer in one mission, supplier in another, distributor in a third and institutional participant in a fourth.

**Non-negotiable architecture rule:** New corridors should be configured through existing market, route, workflow, policy and capability definitions. New country-specific business applications, duplicated databases or forked mission engines are not acceptable.

This is global configurability, not a claim that Corridra can currently deliver services everywhere.

Operational activation always requires real people, permitted data sources, approved communication channels, legal readiness and accountable service delivery.

## 4. The Three Engines

Corridra consists of three interconnected capabilities.

### Engine 1: Cross-Border Mission Infrastructure

The core operational and revenue-producing engine.

It converts a commercial objective into a structured, accountable mission.

Typical lifecycle:

Intent → Requirement → Market → Counterparty Discovery → Qualification → Access → Engagement/Meeting/Visit → Commercial Opportunity → RFQ/Sample/Quotation → Proposal/Commitment → Contract → Invoice → Cash → Repeat

Not every mission requires every step.

A sourcing opportunity may legitimately begin from an RFQ or supplier quotation without a held meeting.

A market-entry mission may terminate successfully with a validated decision not to enter.

The engine manages:

- Mission scope and commercial intent.
- Customer requirements.
- Relevant markets and counterparties.
- Claims, sources, evidence and assessments.
- Relationships and introductions.
- Meetings, factory/site visits and trip agendas.
- Quotations and comparisons.
- Commercial decisions and follow-up.
- Transaction observations and outcomes.
- Responsibility, status, deadlines and audit history.

### Engine 2: Commercial Outcome Graph

Built automatically from real operating activity.

It connects:

Organisation ↔ Person ↔ Product/Capability ↔ Requirement ↔ Market ↔ Mission ↔ Counterparty ↔ Evidence ↔ Relationship ↔ Interaction ↔ Meeting/Visit ↔ Opportunity ↔ Commercial Record ↔ Contribution ↔ Outcome

The purpose is not to draw an impressive network graph.

The purpose is to accumulate reusable knowledge:

- Who was relevant and why.
- Which introductions generated genuine engagement.
- What buyers requested.
- Which supplier evidence mattered.
- Why targets were rejected.
- Which meetings produced opportunities.
- Which opportunities failed and why.
- Which contributors reliably completed valuable work.
- What progressed into documented transactions.
- Which processes reduced delivery cost.

This should remain an ordinary, permissioned relational/event-based system initially. No separate graph database or proprietary prediction engine is required.

Future intelligence must be supported by real outcome data, permitted data rights and measured accuracy.

### Engine 3: Commercial Execution Network

Originally conceived as Diaspora Commerce Grid (DCG).

The broader and more durable definition is a distributed network of people and organisations capable of performing useful cross-border commercial work.

Diaspora remains an important acquisition and trust channel, not the exclusive eligibility criterion.

Potential participants:

- Diaspora professionals.
- Local sourcing agents.
- Trade specialists.
- Factory visit coordinators.
- Commercial connectors.
- Former buyers/procurement professionals.
- Interpreters and translators.
- Technical specialists.
- Researchers.
- Local representatives.
- Institutional partners.
- Accredited verification providers.

Lifecycle:

Mission demand → task definition → eligible participant → accepted assignment → execution → submitted evidence → independent review → contribution attribution → reward/reputation

Task scope, confidentiality, eligibility and compensation must be clear before acceptance.

Private connectors need relationship protection and selective disclosure.

No recruitment pyramid, uncontrolled community expansion, passive-income promise or automatic international payouts.

## 6. First Two Corridors: Asymmetric Launch

Corridra starts with two corridors, deliberately emphasizing different customer problems.

### Corridor A: Bangladesh ↔ China

Primary commercial emphasis: Bangladesh → China.

Focus:

- Product sourcing.
- Machinery/equipment procurement.
- Chinese supplier discovery.
- Supplier quotation comparison.
- Factory/showroom visits.
- Canton Fair and other exhibition-linked missions.
- Guangzhou, Foshan, Shenzhen, Yiwu and other relevant commercial clusters.
- Business buying trips.
- Procurement and trade delegations.
- Private-label/OEM/ODM supplier engagement.

Core proposition: **Go to China knowing which suppliers, products, factories and commercial terms deserve your attention.**

China machinery missions warrant particular attention because buyers may need to assess technical specifications, installation, commissioning, warranties, spare parts, training and long-term service.

The platform should support comparable offers based on actual specifications and commercial terms.

Two quotations must not be treated as equivalent if one excludes critical costs or technical obligations.

China → Bangladesh uses the same system for:

- Distributor/dealer search.
- Customer discovery.
- Industrial partnerships.
- Market validation.
- Local commercial representation assessment.
- Selected business delegations.

This direction is initially secondary and selectively activated.

Pure tourism, visas, leisure travel and hotel booking are not Corridra products. Relevant commercial logistics may be coordinated through external partners.

### Corridor B: Bangladesh ↔ Spain/EU

Primary commercial emphasis: Bangladesh → Spain/EU.

Focus:

- Export buyer access.
- Distributor/customer search.
- Trade-fair effectiveness.
- Foreign-market meetings.
- Commercial-trip planning.
- Buyer qualification.
- RFQ/sample/proposal follow-through.
- Export promotion and institutional programmes.

Core proposition: **Know who is worth meeting before entering the market, including relevant companies outside the event attendee list.**

Spain/EU → Bangladesh supports:

- Bangladesh market validation.
- Distributor/customer discovery.
- Supplier sourcing.
- Industrial/commercial partnership search.
- Local meetings and execution.

The Spain/EU corridor is commercially important in its own right and supports the ambition to establish a genuine Spain-based cross-border technology business.

Spanish presence, ownership, innovation and economic activity must reflect actual operations. Immigration or startup eligibility must never be represented as guaranteed.

### Why the two-corridor strategy matters

China tests supply-side complexity: many potential suppliers, technical qualification, physical sourcing and procurement execution.

Spain/EU tests demand-side scarcity: identifying relevant buyers, creating qualified access and progressing commercial relationships.

Proving both through one foundation demonstrates real product reusability.

Equal feature parity or equal GTM expenditure is not required.

## 7. Universal Mission Object

The Mission is the central organising object.

It should represent:

- Customer/tenant and authorized users.
- Commercial purpose and direction.
- Origin, target and physical locations where applicable.
- Corridor/route and configuration version.
- Mission type and context.
- Product, requirement or offered capability.
- Dates, deadlines and commercial windows.
- Required counterparties.
- Responsible operator.
- Evidence requirements.
- Engagements, visits and quotations.
- Open decisions and next actions.
- Commercial progression.
- Outcome and closure reason.
- Costs and service economics.

Global security and truth rules remain universal.

Corridor-specific rules determine allowable local workflows, data sources, language, communication methods, evidence templates and execution coverage.

A new mission should not require a new company registration or complete new profile when reusable, permitted information already exists.

## 8. Priority User Journeys

### Bangladesh exporter

Provides product, capacity, target market, business objectives and optional travel details.

Receives a qualified target process, relevant introductions/meetings, evidence-aware assessment, follow-up visibility and documented commercial progress.

### Bangladesh importer sourcing from China

Provides requirement, product/category, quantity, budget, specifications and optional trip details.

Receives supplier research, qualification, original quotations, comparisons, visit coordination and structured procurement decisions.

### Machinery buyer

Provides intended application, required capacity, technical and commercial constraints.

Receives comparable machinery options, technical evidence gaps, factory/engineering visit support and commercial follow-through.

### EU/Spanish company entering Bangladesh

Provides offering, preferred customer/channel and market-entry objectives.

Receives structured market assessment, potential customers/distributors, local validation, qualified access and accountable execution.

### EU sourcing buyer

Provides exact supplier requirement and screening criteria.

Receives a shortlist with reasons, uncertainties, relevant evidence and visit/engagement support.

### Chinese supplier

Receives a specific permitted requirement or introduction.

Can indicate relevance, ask for details and respond within authorized scope.

No mandatory registration merely to consider an introduction.

### Institution/delegation leader

Defines the programme and manages a group.

Each participant has an independent commercial objective, protected information and mission outcomes.

The organiser sees permitted programme activity and aggregate outcomes without gaining unrestricted access to private participant data.

### Local contributor

Receives a precisely scoped task.

Sees eligibility, deadline, expected evidence, reward and status.

Can submit work, receive review, inspect attribution and dispute decisions.

### Corridra operator

Manages missions, targets, evidence, engagements, blocked work, deadlines and commercial progression.

The system must make the next useful action obvious.

### Corridra administrator

Manages approved routes, configuration, role access, operational readiness, service economics, AI providers/tasks, contributors, policies and system health.

The administrator must see what is working, what is uncertain, what is blocked, why, and who is accountable.

## 9. Trust and Evidence Constitution

Never confuse:

- Self-declaration.
- Source record.
- Claim.
- Supporting evidence.
- Independent verification.
- Derived inference.
- Recommendation.
- Observed commercial outcome.

Every important commercial fact must preserve source, method, scope, version, review status and relevant dates.

Original language, supplier quotations, currencies, documents and attribution must remain intact.

Translations and AI interpretations are derivative.

A supplier claiming a production capacity does not mean Corridra verified it.

A scheduled meeting does not mean it occurred.

An RFQ does not mean a purchase.

A contract does not mean collected cash.

An external professional audit is different from a Corridra operator's site observation.

Credibility comes from faithfully exposing these distinctions.

## 10. Money and Commercial Truth

Four economic domains must remain separate:

- **A. Customer commercial value:** third-party RFQs, quotations, contracts, invoices and cash outcomes.
- **B. Corridra revenue:** service fees, paid missions, retainers and actual receipts earned by Corridra.
- **C. Corridra delivery costs:** staff time, data, operators, contributors, field work, AI and providers.
- **D. Contributor obligations:** task rewards, approved amounts, reversals and paid status.

Do not present customer trade value as Corridra revenue.

Maintain original currencies and exact amounts.

Do not sum unrelated currencies without an explicit, dated conversion methodology.

Preserve immutable historical financial records and compensating corrections.

No invented accounting confirmations, financial success or trade attribution.

## 11. Business Model

Initial monetisation is primarily paid managed commercial missions.

Working price hypotheses, not validated universal prices:

- Focused mission: approximately €1,500 to €3,500.
- Complex sourcing/market-entry mission: approximately €4,000 to €10,000+.
- Institutional programme: approximately €15,000 to €50,000+.
- Field work: scoped add-on or bundled.
- Ongoing local market support: later recurring retainer.
- Success-linked fees: selective, transparent and legally reviewed.
- Institutional software/intelligence: only after evidence of repeat use and genuine value.

China customers may need different, more accessible pricing tiers in BDT and category-specific economics. Do not mechanically apply European price assumptions to small Bangladeshi importers.

Pricing should reflect mission difficulty, service scope, customer alternatives, qualified access, evidence needs, human workload and commercial value.

The main commercial threat is becoming an expensive consulting agency with software.

Track:

Service revenue minus attributable delivery costs equals mission contribution.

Also track revenue per human delivery hour and how repeatable workflows reduce time and cost.

A better interface alone is not evidence of improving economics.

- Bilateral commercial organisations.
- Selective trade agencies and institutional partners.

## 13. Natural Growth Loops

Corridra should spread through useful commercial interactions rather than consumer-style referral gimmicks.

Key loops:

- **Buyer loop:** paid seller mission → buyer receives useful introduction → buyer later creates sourcing intent.
- **Supplier loop:** verified buying requirement → targeted suppliers respond → suppliers discover Corridra's market-access offerings.
- **Institutional loop:** one programme → multiple companies → individual missions → repeat programmes.
- **Execution Network loop:** real paid work → qualified contributors → stronger corridor coverage → more deliverable missions.
- **Outcome loop:** more missions → better evidence/outcomes → stronger targeting → higher customer value → repeat missions.
- **Multi-corridor loop:** a company sourcing machinery from China may later sell its manufactured products in Europe, reusing its commercial profile and approved evidence.

No mandatory address-book import, spam, recruitment commissions or artificial network growth.

## 14. Competitive Positioning

Corridra competes with combinations of:

- In-house export/procurement teams.
- Trade agencies and chambers.
- Market-entry operators such as ALTIOS and OCO Global.
- Local sourcing and commercial agents.
- Hosted-buyer programmes such as Wabel/ECRM.
- Event matching platforms such as Grip, Swapcard and b2match.
- Marketplaces and sourcing platforms.
- Data tools and contact intelligence.
- Existing manual Excel/email/WeChat/WhatsApp workflows.

Many competitors already perform parts of the job extremely well.

Corridra should leverage them where possible.

Intended differentiation:

One mission-centric infrastructure coordinating intelligence, qualification, trusted access, physical execution, follow-through, evidence and outcome learning.

Current differentiation is a product thesis and architecture.

A competitive moat is not established merely because the code exists.

Potential future advantages:

- Corridor execution density.
- Repeated qualified buyer access.
- Strong relationship provenance.
- Reusable supplier/technical evidence.
- Trusted local operators.
- Outcome-labelled commercial history.
- Better qualification and reduced wasted work.
- Institutional embedding and switching costs.

These must be earned through real transactions, trust and measured repeatability.

## 15. Governed AI

AI is an operational capability, not Corridra's product identity.

Corridra should maintain a single provider-neutral AI Gateway.

The administrator can route supported tasks among approved Gemini, OpenAI, Anthropic or future models without modifying business-domain logic.

Changing an existing configured provider/model should not require rebuilding the entire application. New provider capabilities may require adapter development and review.

AI tasks include:

- Commercial-intent structuring.
- Requirement extraction.
- Company research assistance.
- Entity-resolution suggestions.
- Supplier/buyer fit explanation.
- Machinery specification extraction.
- Quotation extraction and comparison assistance.
- Document gap analysis.
- Translation.
- Meeting briefs.
- Follow-up suggestions.
- Opportunity summaries.

AI outputs remain drafts/inferences with review and provenance.

AI cannot independently authorize access, verify commercial facts, approve money, execute payouts, send consequential messages, make legal decisions, accept binding commercial terms or destroy protected records.

Retrieval permissions must be enforced before model access.

Provider location, privacy terms, cost ceilings, token usage, evaluation results and policy versions must be controlled.

The core product remains usable when AI is unavailable.

## 16. Security, Privacy and Operating Standards

Corridra will handle commercially sensitive and potentially personal information.

Requirements include:

- Tenant isolation and PostgreSQL RLS.
- Least-privilege, server-enforced authorization.
- Separate customer, staff/operator and contributor access.
- MFA for privileged operations.
- Evidence classification, purpose and retention.
- Source-rights/licensing control.
- Secure object storage and malware scanning.
- Auditable configuration changes.
- Immutable material events.
- Transactional outbox and idempotency.
- Safe retry and workflow recovery.
- Restricted, expiring sharing links.
- Controlled international data transfers.
- Legal-hold and deletion/DSAR processes.
- Privacy-safe logs and monitoring.
- Backups and tested recovery.
- Human approvals for sensitive actions.

EU-hosted infrastructure does not automatically establish compliance in every corridor.

Route readiness must be independently evaluated.

Built, tested, deployed, enabled, legally approved and commercially operational are different statuses.

Never collapse them into one "complete" label.

## 17. UX and Product Identity

Corridra should be exceptionally clear, composed, fast, trustworthy and efficient.

Approved direction combines:

- **Version 10, Quiet Intelligence:** overarching coherent experience.
- **Version 07, SourceLab:** rigorous comparisons, evidence and technical precision.
- **Version 01, Heritage Atlas:** editorial credibility and distinctive presentation.
- **Version 02, Precision Ledger:** dense, efficient operational workflows.

These are one unified design language, not four selectable themes or separate applications.

Public visitors navigate primarily by commercial job, not backend modules.

The experience should prioritize:

- Immediate comprehension.
- Task-first entry.
- Low-friction progressive intake.
- Clear relevance and commercial context.
- Explicit evidence and uncertainty.
- Simple buyer/supplier responses.
- Understandable mission progression.
- Fast operator actions.
- Meaningful admin visibility.
- Excellent mobile behavior.
- Accessible keyboard and assistive interactions.
- Honest loading, empty, error, blocked and recovery states.

Avoid fake performance metrics, generic global-tech imagery, fabricated testimonials, decorative dashboards, meaningless AI scores and excessive workflow complexity.

Every CTA must do what it says.

Complete functional coverage is more important than a visual mockup.

Never sacrifice existing authorized actions, data fidelity, accessibility or security to achieve a particular visual composition.

## 23. The Product Decision Test

Every proposed feature, change, partner or new corridor should answer:

- What real commercial problem does this solve?
- Who needs it and who will pay?
- What does the user do today instead?
- Does this increase relevant access, trust, execution quality or measurable progress?
- Can existing infrastructure or a partner provide the capability more cheaply?
- Can it reuse Corridra's global primitives without country-specific duplication?
- Does it improve mission economics or generate permitted compounding knowledge?
- Can it be delivered, secured, operated and supported reliably?

If the answer is weak, defer or reject it.

Corridra's intended destination is not a collection of international trade tools. It is a reusable operating infrastructure that makes difficult cross-border commercial work more deliberate, productive, trustworthy, measurable and economically efficient.
