# CORRIDRA V3

The Complete Product & Experience Contract

Edition: Intelligent Simplicity — Apple-level Product Discipline
Status: Final proposed V3 specification for implementation
Baseline reviewed: "sadat99/corridra" · "main" · "e9ed2e5" · 16 October 2026

North Star: Make international commercial relationships easier to establish, qualify, manage and convert into measurable business outcomes.

Design principle: As simple as possible for the user. As comprehensive as necessary underneath.

## 1. The Definitive Product

Corridra is a globally configurable cross-border commercial access, facilitation and delegation platform.

It helps businesses identify, qualify, meet and transact with relevant international counterparties. It also empowers independent organisers and institutions to run commercially valuable delegations efficiently and profitably.

Its purpose is not to provide company directories, more contacts or another complicated management platform.

Its purpose is to help customers answer:

- Who should we do business with?
- Why are they relevant and credible?
- How can we meet or engage them?
- What must happen for business to progress?
- What results did we actually achieve?

### Three customer-facing product families

| Product                             | Customer's job                                                         | Scope                                                                                                      |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Find Buyers & Business Partners  | Sell, distribute, enter markets and establish commercial relationships | Buyer/distributor qualification, B2B meetings, exhibitions, business visits, representation and deals      |
| 2. Find Suppliers & Source Products | Buy, procure, manufacture or secure supply                             | Manufacturers, machinery, finished goods, materials, OEM, screening, RFQs, factory visits and transactions |
| 3. Delegation OS                    | Organise, lead, manage or participate in commercial delegations        | Recruitment, participant requirements, fees, programmes, meetings, local execution and outcomes            |

These are three clear entry experiences, not three disconnected applications.

A fourth, secondary entry — Other international business objective — accommodates market-entry assessments, bilateral partnerships, special projects and emerging use cases without inventing another primary product.

### Essential unification

A manufacturer is a company that manufactures. Depending on the transaction, it can be a supplier, buyer, seller, customer, distributor or commercial partner.

A supplier is not necessarily a manufacturer. It may be a trading company, distributor, service provider or other commercial entity.

Consequently:

- Maintain one canonical organisation identity within permitted tenant and data-rights boundaries.
- Represent commercial roles contextually, not as mutually exclusive identities.
- Do not duplicate company records solely because the company participates in buying and selling.
- Reuse qualification, meetings, evidence, relationships and commercial progression across selling and sourcing.
- Allow the same company to participate in multiple roles, routes, missions and delegations.

The underlying shared commercial engine is more important than the three navigation choices.

## 2. Complete Commercial Workflows

### Product 1 — Find Buyers & Business Partners

Typical customer request: “I am attending an exhibition in Spain. Help me arrange relevant buyer meetings.”

Also supports international buyers or suppliers visiting Bangladesh, independent business trips, inbound delegations and other corridors.

Complete journey

1. Define offering, capacity, commercial objectives and target markets.
2. Identify event, intended visit, preferred dates and meeting location when relevant.
3. Establish target buyer/distributor/customer profiles.
4. Research and discover potentially relevant companies.
5. Assess commercial fit, capabilities, available evidence and limitations.
6. Build and approve a shortlist.
7. Facilitate permitted introductions and meeting requests.
8. Coordinate mutually accepted meeting times, locations, participants and agendas.
9. Record actual engagement, attendance and outcomes.
10. Facilitate RFQs, samples, proposals, negotiations, contracts and follow-through.
11. Maintain the relationship and support repeat commercial opportunities.

Meeting-related statuses must distinguish identified, qualified, approached, interested, proposed, confirmed, held, declined, cancelled and follow-up pending, wherever applicable.

Do not represent an introduction request as an accepted meeting or a held meeting as a signed deal.

An event is useful context, not a restriction on which counterparties can be researched.

Market representation, local partnership development and market-entry validation are variations of the same commercial-access capability.

### Product 2 — Find Suppliers & Source Products

Typical customer request: “I need qualified manufacturers in China for a particular product or machine.”

Complete journey

1. Define product or procurement objective.
2. Capture relevant technical, commercial, regulatory and delivery requirements.
3. Discover manufacturers, traders or other eligible suppliers.
4. Screen commercial relevance and available evidence.
5. Identify missing information, risks and verification needs.
6. Shortlist and request quotations or further information.
7. Preserve original supplier responses and quotation revisions.
8. Compare commercial and technical suitability.
9. Arrange permitted factory, supplier, showroom or technical meetings.
10. Facilitate samples, trials, negotiations, commercial decisions and orders.
11. Record procurement progression and subsequent outcomes.
12. Retain authorized relationship and supplier knowledge for future sourcing.

Context-dependent sourcing capabilities

| Sourcing context         | Additional requirements surfaced                                                                                                     |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Machinery/equipment      | Application, capacity, power, installation, commissioning, acceptance tests, warranties, spares, maintenance and after-sales support |
| Finished products        | Specifications, quality, packaging, certifications where applicable, MOQ, lead times and delivery                                    |
| Raw materials/components | Grades, tolerances, consistency, testing, supply continuity and traceability                                                         |
| OEM/custom manufacturing | Drawings, tooling, samples, IP/confidentiality, production capacity and quality acceptance                                           |
| Other procurement        | Relevant category-specific criteria under reviewed configuration                                                                     |

No separate machinery application or duplicated sourcing engine.

Critical: A low quoted price must never conceal missing costs, different technical scope, installation obligations, uncertain certification or incompatible payment/delivery terms.

Original quotations, currencies, specifications, source language and evidence remain accessible. Derived comparisons must disclose assumptions.

### Product 3 — Delegation OS

Typical customer request: “I organise commercial delegations. Help me operate more efficiently, improve participant outcomes and protect my margin.”

This product serves independent organisers, entrepreneurs, chambers, associations, trade bodies, business groups and institutional operators.

Organiser journey

1. Create a delegation for a supported destination, market, event or programme.
2. Define objectives, sectors, dates, eligibility, capacity and participation terms.
3. Establish participant pricing, expected expenses and commercial objectives.
4. Publish controlled invitations and register participants.
5. Collect individual company profiles, needs and commercial intentions.
6. Review eligibility, confirmations and participation status.
7. Build shared programme activities, agendas, visits and meetings.
8. Arrange qualified individual commercial engagements using Corridra's shared commercial engines.
9. Assign responsibilities, local support and coordination tasks.
10. Manage participant communications, schedules and changes.
11. Record costs, payments and organiser economics where authorized.
12. Track participant activity, consented outcomes and overall programme results.
13. Produce scoped reporting, feedback and repeatable programme templates.

Essential product model

Organiser workspace → Multiple delegations → Participants → Independent commercial missions → Shared programme and individual outcomes.

Delegations must not become a completely separate backend mission system.

A participant may be seeking buyers, suppliers, manufacturers, distributors or other commercial partners. These are all supported through the same underlying capabilities.

Participants retain appropriate privacy and control over their individual information.

Organisers see programme activity and expressly permitted commercial information, not automatic access to private offers, contacts, contract values or individual negotiations.

A user may be both an organiser and a participant, or run several programmes concurrently.

Organiser business economics

The platform should help organisers understand:

- Participant fees and payment status.
- Organiser expenses and obligations.
- Corridra platform/service fees.
- Contribution margin per programme.
- Capacity, attendance and participant satisfaction.
- Qualified meetings and documented business outcomes.
- Repeat business and programme performance.

Accounting separation is mandatory: Organiser revenue is not Corridra revenue. Participant trade values are not organiser revenue. Contributor obligations and supplier payments are not automatically Corridra expenses.

Fees, refunds, cancellations and payment collections require explicit contracting, provider integration and applicable legal review before activation.

Corridra is not automatically the travel operator, merchant of record, visa provider or party responsible for a participant's commercial transaction.

## 3. One Platform, Multiple Ways to Work

All three products run on one globally configurable foundation.

### Shared engines

A. Commercial Mission Infrastructure

Requirements, markets, organisations, qualification, introductions, meetings, visits, quotations, commitments, commercial progression and accountability.

Support valid opportunities that begin from RFQs, quotations or other documented origins without inventing meetings.

Not every objective needs every workflow stage, and a validated decision not to proceed can be a useful outcome.

B. Commercial Outcome Graph

An evidence-aware, permissioned accumulation of companies, relationships, activities, decisions, contributions, outcomes and reusable knowledge.

Use existing relational/event infrastructure initially. A separate graph database is not required.

C. Commercial Execution Network

Invited and approved professionals, local operators, interpreters, technical specialists, researchers, introducers and diaspora participants delivering scoped tasks.

Retain controlled assignment, confidentiality, evidence, review, reward and dispute handling.

The network is shared infrastructure and an optional paid delivery capability, not a mandatory fourth customer-facing application.

Additional shared capabilities

- Corridor Control Center and versioned configurations.
- Evidence, provenance, rights and verification.
- Organisation and identity management.
- Role-scoped communications and sharing.
- Governed AI provider/task abstraction.
- Commercial reporting and service economics.
- Durable workflow, audit, recovery and operational monitoring.

### Global configurability

Support country-to-country directions using one architecture, with configurable markets, route packs, workflows, eligibility, requirements and capabilities.

Preserve distinctions among:

- Country and trade bloc.
- Commercial demand origin and target market.
- Supplier/manufacturer location.
- Meeting or event location.
- Manufacturing location.
- Shipment origin and destination.
- Governing jurisdiction and service coverage.

Bangladesh → China and China → Bangladesh remain independently configured commercial directions.

Initial commercial focus:

- Bangladesh → China: Sourcing, machinery, manufacturer qualification, meetings and delegations.
- Bangladesh → Spain/EU: Export buyer access, fair-related matchmaking, distributor development and follow-through.
- Inbound to Bangladesh: Qualified buyer/supplier/partner meetings and related commercial support.
- Other markets/directions: Configurable where supported, activated only when actually ready.

A selectable country is not a promise of local service availability.

## 4. The V3 Experience System

The product should follow Apple's best principles of clarity, consistency, restraint, craftsmanship and progressive disclosure while preserving Corridra's own brand identity.

The experience has three levels of depth, not three duplicated applications.

### Level 1 — Guided

For new users and occasional customers.

Shows:

- The current objective.
- Only essential questions.
- Relevant options.
- What will happen next.
- The next useful result.

No unnecessary system vocabulary or configuration.

### Level 2 — Professional

For experienced companies and delegation organisers.

Shows:

- Multiple concurrent projects.
- Relationship and company portfolios.
- Shortlists, comparisons and original evidence.
- Meetings and schedules.
- Commercial progress, finances and reporting.
- Filters, bulk actions and reusable workflows.

### Level 3 — Operations & Administration

For authorized staff, administrators and founder.

Shows:

- Complete case and programme operations.
- Technical review and exception handling.
- Corridor/policy configuration.
- Financial and contributor controls.
- Permissioned AI operations.
- Security, audit, readiness, reliability and recovery.

These are progressive exposure levels, not bypasses of role-based authorization.

A simple-mode user can access entitled detail when needed. An unauthorized user cannot expose information by switching modes.

### Entry experience

The public homepage presents three prominent choices:

Find Buyers & Partners
Find Suppliers & Products
Organise a Delegation

A secondary link allows other commercial objectives.

No signup is needed merely to understand the service or explore permissible examples. Real information submission remains subject to approved admission rules.

### Guided request

The system asks for the minimum required to produce a meaningful next step:

- What the user wants.
- Relevant country/market.
- Necessary commercial details.
- Event/visit/delegation context, if applicable.
- Desired assistance.
- Essential contact/identity information when justified.

Save or reuse authorized information. Support draft continuation where permitted, safe retries and clear recovery.

Do not demand a full organisational profile, arbitrary document uploads or mandatory AI inference before providing a legitimate initial result.

### Persistent customer workspaces

Customers need lasting workspaces, not just isolated mission receipts.

Recommended primary navigation:

Home · My Work · Companies · Results · More

Within My Work, relevant records may be missions, sourcing engagements, meetings, delegations or other supported commercial activities.

Company relationships and authorized reusable supplier/buyer information remain accessible across engagements.

### The contextual workspace pattern

Every commercial engagement should have a consistent structure:

Overview

- What is the goal?
- What is happening?
- What needs attention?
- Who is responsible?

Companies

- Relevant counterparties.
- Qualification.
- Evidence and relationship context.

Meetings & Activities

- Introductions.
- Meetings.
- Visits.
- Tasks and programme activities.

Commercial Progress

- RFQs.
- Quotations and comparisons.
- Proposals.
- Commitments.
- Contracts and financial observations.

Details & History

- Requirements.
- Source records and versions.
- Permissions.
- Original documents.
- Audit and decisions.
- Advanced tools.

These are contextual sections, not compulsory tabs on every page.

A simple buyer mission should not display machinery commissioning controls. A machinery mission must expose them when relevant.

Do not hide material risks, legal disclosures or commercial exclusions purely to reduce visual density.

### Audience-specific workspaces

| Audience                    | Experience                                                                    |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Exporter / seller           | Customers, buyer access, meetings, opportunities and results                  |
| Importer / procurement team | Suppliers, requirements, quotations, technical decisions and procurement      |
| Delegation organiser        | Delegations, participants, agendas, meetings, programme finances and outcomes |
| Delegation participant      | Programme, individual meetings, own mission and private results               |

| Local contributor           | Assigned tasks, deadlines, submissions, reviews and rewards                   |
| Corridra operator           | Today, Missions, Companies, Delivery and Outcomes                             |
| Founder/admin               | Business performance, operations, corridors, people, governance and readiness |

A person with multiple authorized roles may switch context without having to manage duplicate accounts unnecessarily. Existing identity security boundaries must remain intact.

### The founder's clarity layer

Provide a dedicated authorized view answering:

1. What can Corridra currently do?
2. What is actually activated?
3. What are customers and operators doing?
4. What is producing commercial results and revenue?
5. What is blocked, why, and who must act?

Include an explicitly labelled synthetic product explorer to demonstrate representative journeys safely.

Never fabricate live KPIs from demonstration data.

## 7. Visual, Interaction & Cognitive Simplicity Standard

V3 adopts Apple-level product discipline, not an Apple imitation.

### Visual system

Preserve Corridra's existing brand foundation:

- Ivory-based backgrounds.
- Dark ink typography.
- Forest-green identity and restrained accents.
- Accessible contrast and clear semantic states.
- Consistent spacing, typography and component geometry.
- Subtle borders and purposeful surface elevation.
- High-quality, restrained motion.
- Native-feeling responsive interactions.
- International-language and original-script support.

Use typography and hierarchy to distinguish:

1. What this screen is about.
2. What matters now.
3. What the user should do.
4. What details can be explored.

Professional comparison tables may be information-dense. They should not be aesthetically minimized at the cost of useful data.

### Interaction laws

1. One clear primary task per screen. Secondary tasks remain accessible.
2. At most five top-level navigation choices per audience, with contextual subnavigation.
3. Progressive disclosure rather than feature deletion.
4. Relevant information beside the relevant decision.
5. No forced journey stages that are commercially unnecessary.
6. No fabricated AI insight, verification, performance claim or success status.
7. No unnecessary repeated input.
8. No hidden failure, silent data loss or unexplained permissions error.
9. No role-switching leakage or shared private state between tenants.
10. Advanced expert paths remain discoverable and efficient.

### User-facing terminology

Use simple, internationally comprehensible commercial language.

Keep precise trade terms where needed: quotation, MOQ, lead time, payment terms, delivery terms, warranty, RFQ and similar concepts.

Explain uncommon terms at first use.

Distinguish clearly:

- Request versus accepted engagement.
- Proposed versus confirmed meeting.
- Confirmed versus held meeting.
- Self-declared versus verified evidence.
- AI draft versus reviewed conclusion.
- Quote versus order.
- Contract versus invoice.
- Invoice versus confirmed payment.
- Customer trade value versus Corridra revenue.
- Product implemented versus commercially activated.

### Performance and accessibility

- Support keyboard, touch and pointer interaction.
- Preserve access at mobile/tablet/desktop widths.
- Meet applicable WCAG 2.2 AA requirements.
- Support reduced motion.
- Avoid heavy animations, unnecessary loading and disruptive navigation.
- Preserve unsaved work appropriately through navigation, interruptions and errors.
- Design for variable bandwidth and internationally distributed users.

No arbitrary 10× speed, performance or conversion claim is permitted without actual measurement.

## 8. Commercial Model, Governance & Safety

### Revenue opportunities

Near-term:

- Qualified counterparty research and meeting facilitation.
- Managed sourcing and manufacturer qualification.
- Machinery procurement assistance.
- Paid commercial missions and institutional programmes.
- Approved local execution and follow-through.

Emerging SaaS:

- Delegation OS per-programme or recurring organizer plans.
- Business/customer commercial workspace subscriptions.
- Usage-based or paid managed assistance where economically justified.

Later:

- Institutional/enterprise corridor operations.
- Commercial intelligence using rights-permitted, sufficiently validated data.
- Lawfully structured, transparently disclosed success or referral fees.

Pricing remains to be validated through real customer research and paid engagements.

### Financial truth

Keep separate:

1. The customer's underlying commercial transaction value.
2. The delegation organiser's own revenue, costs and obligations.
3. Corridra's actual service/platform revenue.
4. Corridra's attributable delivery costs.
5. Contributor compensation obligations.

Do not double-count financial flows. Record original currencies exactly. Derived conversions require sourced rates and explicit dates and rounding methods.

### Operating truth

A feature may be:
Designed → Implemented → Tested → Deployed → Enabled → Legally/operationally approved → Commercially live.

These are different states.

At the audited baseline, Corridra remains synthetic-only for commercial missions. Real-data admission, real outreach, production AI inference, contributor payments and commercial deployment require their independent security, legal, provider and readiness approvals.

Existing external control gates D01–D06 and D08 remain independently binding until closed with evidence.

No UI change, account signup, deployment or test pass overrides those gates.

### Product boundaries

Corridra is not primarily:

- A generic B2B directory.
- A commodity CRM.
- An Alibaba replacement.
- A complete procurement ERP.
- A trade-compliance or freight platform.
- A travel, hotel or visa agency.
- A bank, payment processor or lender.
- A spam/outreach engine.
- An unrestricted gig marketplace.
- An autonomous negotiator.

Integrations or specialist partnerships are preferable when adjacent functions are necessary but do not create proprietary differentiation.

## 9. Mandatory Implementation and Acceptance Protocol

### Phase 0 — Establish the actual baseline

Before changing V3 code:

- Fetch latest "main", active PRs and deployed SHA.
- Read "AGENTS.md", the latest accepted owner specifications, current plan, implementation matrix, ADRs and UX10X preservation documents.
- Inventory actual routes, components, controls, commands, APIs, migrations, roles, permissions, domain invariants, exports and tests.
- Reconcile the dated UX coverage ledgers with current code.
- Identify every existing user action and meaningful state.
- Mark each requirement as EXISTING, PARTIAL, MISSING, GATED, DEFERRED or NOT VERIFIED.
- Record baseline screenshots and core journey tests.
- Map all existing functionality into the intended V3 experience.

Do not treat old ledger totals or synthetic test counts as current acceptance evidence.

Required output: An exhaustive, traceable V2 → V3 feature/action/state mapping, including the three products, other use cases, all audience roles and all protected operations.

### Phase 1 — Unified experience foundation

Implement:

- A single consistent V3 design system.
- Role-aware navigation and contextual workspaces.
- Guided commercial objective entry.
- Clear customer/operator/founder landing experiences.
- Consistent status, errors, recovery and detail disclosure.
- Backward-compatible access to existing authorized functionality.

Do not rebuild the backend to solve navigation problems.

### Phase 2 — Three complete flagship journeys

Implement and verify the user experiences for:

A. Buyer access and international meetings.
B. Supplier sourcing including machinery.
C. Independent commercial delegation operation.

Reuse existing backend capabilities wherever appropriate.

Mark truly missing backend requirements explicitly; never fake completeness with interface-only controls.

Each journey must support the intended self-service, managed or hybrid model when its required functionality and activation gates are actually ready.

### Phase 3 — Missing SaaS infrastructure

Implement separately reviewed additions for:

- Organisation/tenant provisioning.
- Authorized memberships and customer workspaces.
- Self-service delegation administration.
- Participant flows and required organiser economics.
- Notifications and permitted communications.
- Commercial packaging and billing when justified.

Preserve existing customer/operator identity separation, RLS and protected source disclosures. Use additive, backward-compatible migrations where needed.

### Phase 4 — Founder controls and end-to-end verification

Complete:

- Clear founder operational visibility.
- Real versus synthetic status.
- Product and service availability.
- Verified business and service economics.
- Advanced administration.
- Role-specific customer journey walkthroughs.
- Full coverage and regression testing.

### Phase 5 — Commercial activation

Independently close the approved:

- Legal/privacy and contractual prerequisites.
- Real-data processing and retention controls.
- Scanning and storage requirements.
- Communication-provider and outreach restrictions.
- Security, recovery and operational assurance.
- Payment/provider requirements for the selected business model.
- Route-specific staffing and delivery capability.
- Paid pilot acceptance.

Activate only approved services, routes, roles and capabilities.

### Per-PR acceptance

Every PR must identify:

- Baseline and tested commit.
- Changed requirement IDs.
- Existing versus new functionality.
- Original and destination UI paths.
- Changed server/domain contracts, if any.
- Positive tests and permission-denial tests.
- Loading, empty, conflict, error, retry, revoked/expired and interrupted states.
- Responsive and accessibility outcomes.
- Data/history/financial preservation evidence.
- Remaining gaps, blockers and feature flags.
- Deployment and rollback compatibility.
- Exact result: verified, partial, blocked or unverified.

A cosmetic redesign cannot be called a complete product upgrade.

### Objective acceptance measurements

Assess representative users on:

- Ability to understand Corridra's purpose and choose the correct starting point.
- Time to first useful result.
- Task completion and error rate.
- Navigation/search effort.
- Ability to resume unfinished work.
- Accuracy and discoverability of advanced capabilities.
- Time and human effort required for commercial execution.
- Quality of buyer/supplier qualification.
- Actual meetings held and commercial progress.
- Organiser economics and participant outcomes.
- Revenue, attributable delivery costs and contribution margins.

Compare against a reproducible V2 baseline where measurable. Targets must be agreed before claiming improvements.

### Hard release conditions

V3 is not accepted if:

- An original authorized function becomes inaccessible.
- Domain authority or privacy weakens.
- Original source, money, evidence or history is lost.
- Important commercial requirements disappear from workflows.
- Delegation organisers cannot complete the explicitly accepted V3 organiser journey once its SaaS release is claimed complete.
- Buyer/supplier role reuse creates incorrect company identities or permissions.
- Advanced operations become undiscoverable.
