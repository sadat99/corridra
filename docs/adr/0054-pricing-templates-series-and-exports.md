# ADR-054: Pricing templates, programme series, repeat revenue and exports

Status: bounded implementation, synthetic only, default off (`CORRIDRA_CUSTOMER_PROGRAMME_PRICING_ENABLED`). V3-06-007 and V3-06-010 stay PARTIAL for the whole product. This module records prices the organiser says they charge and groups repeat editions of a programme. It collects, holds and moves no money, converts no currency and sends nothing.

## Audit: what already existed and what is reused

- **ADR046 organiser economics** (`customer-programme-economics-migrations`): an append-only per-programme ledger of fees, payments, planned and actual costs in exact integer minor units per currency, with compensating entries, a derived per-programme summary and the `participant_scope` authority check. It has no price list, no way to apply one fee schedule to many participants, no view across programmes and no export. This module writes its fees into that same ledger table through the same integrity trigger and reads the same sums.
- **ADR048 programme templates** (`customer-programme-templates-migrations`): versioned, retirable templates holding a purpose, capacity, tasks and planned costs, applied only to an empty programme. It deliberately holds no fees. This module reuses it unchanged to seed every new edition, and adds no second template concept for plans.
- **ADR037 programme creation** (`customer.create_programme`) and **ADR041 participants**: an edition is a normal programme created through the existing command, and fees attach to existing registrations.
- **ADR051 roles**: `owner` and `admin` write; the existing helpers (`programme_actor`, `participant_scope`, `collaborative_workspaces`) already read the append-only role history, so nothing here changes how roles are read.
- Event, audit and outbox rows use the same pattern as every module (one chain per real change, identifiers and counts only).
- **Gap this ADR fills:** fee schedules (tiers, early booking by date, group reductions by participant count), bulk fee recording, series and recurrence, repeat revenue across editions, and workspace exports.

## Decision

Add an optional additive module installed by `migrateCustomerProgrammePricing`. It first installs the templates module (and through it coordination, economics and participants), then eleven private tables and eleven restricted functions.

- **Fee schedules (pricing templates).** A workspace saves named schedules of one currency with 1 to 12 tiers (a Synthetic name and an exact `bigint` amount in minor units), up to 6 early-booking reductions (a last day and an amount) and up to 6 group reductions (a minimum number of participants and an amount). Reductions are fixed amounts, never percentages, so there is no rounding rule to hide. The largest early-booking reduction plus the largest group reduction must stay below the cheapest tier, so no schedule can produce a zero or negative fee. Every change is a new revision with an expected version. A schedule can be retired and restored, never deleted. At most 50 per workspace.
- **Applying a schedule.** Applying a schedule to a programme records a new fee schedule version on that programme, holding an exact copy of the revision. Later edits of the template never change what a programme was given. Applying records no fee by itself.
- **Bulk fees.** One command names participants and a tier for each, plus a fee date. The caller never supplies an amount: the database computes each fee from the programme's current schedule (tier amount, minus the largest early-booking reduction whose last day is not before the fee date, minus the largest group reduction whose size the active participant count reaches). Any invalid row rejects the whole batch with nothing written: unknown tier, a registration outside the programme, a withdrawn or removed participant, a repeated participant, a participant who already has an uncorrected fee, a stale schedule version, a fee that would reach zero, or more than 200 rows. A preview runs the same computation and writes nothing. Recording inserts one `PARTICIPANT_FEE` ledger entry per row through the economics table and its trigger, one batch record, one line per participant pointing at its entry, and one event. The result reports a total per currency. Amounts stay strings end to end and are summed as exact numerics.
- **Series.** A series is a named, versioned plan: a programme template (ADR048), an explicit increasing list of 1 to 24 start dates, and a retired flag. Dates are listed, not generated by rule; the screen offers a helper that fills the list from a first date, a count and a step of one month, three months or one year. A schedule can grow later but can never change or drop the date of an edition that exists. At most 30 series per workspace.
- **Editions.** Creating the next edition is one command that names the series version and template version the organiser saw and the edition number being created. In one transaction it creates a programme through `customer.create_programme`, applies the template through `customer.programme_template_apply` with the next scheduled start date, and links the programme to the series. Settings, tasks and planned costs come from the template. No participant, invitation, fee, payment, actual cost or personal data is copied, because the template cannot hold any. A failure at any step rolls the whole edition back. Series membership is append-only and an edition is in exactly one series.
- **Repeat revenue and margin.** A read-only view over the organiser's own ledger across the editions of one series. Per edition and per currency it shows fees due, payments recorded, outstanding, planned costs, actual costs, margin on actual costs, cash margin and the exact change in fees due since the previous edition when that edition has the same currency. Series totals are per currency. Nothing is converted or summed across currencies, nothing is projected, and the view says it is organiser-recorded and unverified. A series with no editions shows nothing, not zeros.
- **Exports.** Owners and admins can download two CSV files of their own workspace: programmes with participant counts and per-currency totals, and the fees and costs ledger (optionally one programme). The file is generated on the server, bounded (2000 programme rows, 10000 ledger rows; larger asks are refused with a message to narrow the choice), sent with no-store headers and a file name made only of the dataset and the date. Cells that a spreadsheet could read as formulas are prefixed with a quote; genuine numbers are left alone. Participant labels are never exported (only opaque references). Every export writes an immutable record (who, which dataset, how many rows, never the rows) and one event chain. Members, participants, revoked or disabled people and other workspaces are denied.
- **Authority.** One check, `customer_private.pricing_scope`, runs first in every command and read: the existing helpers confirm the fresh proof, admission, workspace, programme and service executor, then the current role must be `owner` or `admin`. Nothing comes from the caller's claim of workspace, role or subject. Members see nothing here because the figures are the organiser's money, matching ADR046.
- **Retries and concurrency.** Each write carries a command id: an exact retry returns the original, a changed payload under the same id is rejected. Writers are serialised per workspace, programme or series, so two simultaneous bulk batches record once and two requests for the same edition create it once.
- **History.** Every table is immutable (`app.reject_rewrite`). Database triggers additionally reject skipped versions, a fee line that does not match its ledger entry, and an edition whose date is not the series date at its position.
- **Events.** Identifiers, versions, counts and the kind only. No name, text or amount.

## Read-only link to qualified meetings: not built

The register asks to show qualified meetings per programme if a safe read path exists. It does not. Meetings and qualified opportunities belong to missions (`app.meeting`, `app.opportunity`), which are linked to imported participants by staff (`app.delivery_participant`). A customer-created programme has independent registrations whose private missions belong to different authorities (ADR042), and ADR042 records that a programme registration or objective summary grants no mission transfer or meeting authority, while ADR043 records that the existing agenda needs imported participants. Inventing a link would either leak a participant's private mission or fabricate qualification. The gap is recorded here and no function reads meetings.

## Synthetic-only boundary

All free text must begin with `Synthetic `. The routes are off unless the workspace, programme, participant, economics, coordination and templates flags and `CORRIDRA_CUSTOMER_PROGRAMME_PRICING_ENABLED` are set, the environment is synthetic, with production requiring the explicit ADR049 synthetic-production opt-in. Real-use admission remains a separate owner-authorized implementation packet. Fee records are the organiser's own unverified bookkeeping. D01 to D06 and D08 stay closed.

## Not included

Collecting or moving money, payment providers, refunds as a flow, invoicing, tax, any currency conversion or rate, percentage discounts, forecasts, automatic recurrence by rule or on a timer, cloning participants, sharing schedules or series across workspaces, a public library, attaching an existing programme to a series, qualified-meeting display (above), scheduled or emailed exports, and any outbound message.

## Blast radius and rollback

New objects only; no existing migration, function, policy or row changes, and a populated before-and-after snapshot test proves it. Rollback is unsetting the flag and redeploying the previous application. The additive schema and everything recorded stay in place and are never dropped. Fee entries already written are ordinary ledger entries and can be corrected with the existing compensating-entry rule.

## Required acceptance

Populated additive install and repeat install with exact registry hash and a history-mismatch refusal; direct SQL denial for runtime and auth logins on every private table, helper and command; owner and admin write, with member, participant, revoked, disabled, outsider and other-workspace denial and forged workspace or programme denial; stale versions; exact retries and changed-payload conflicts; immutability of every history table; event privacy; synthetic-only validation in schema and SQL; exactness above 2^53 and per-currency totals; all-or-nothing batches; concurrent batches and editions; template, series and schedule refusal cases; CSV escaping; keyboard and axe at four widths with the flag off and on.
