# ADR-074: Each translated page has its own release

Status: accepted on 30 October 2026. Proposed the same day with the request-form build (PR 215). The owner merged that pull request (`9e77170`) and accepted the static-host signal ("accept signal, bn & others"). The static-host signal is now an accepted exception to ADR-066 "What the server knows: Nothing", for the request form's words files only. It amends [ADR-066](0066-language-phase-2.md) for the request form's words; otherwise ADR-066 applies as written. The owner wants all six form languages (ar, bn, de, es, fr, zh) released and approved their words the same day ("approve all"). That sets nothing by itself: `CORRIDRA_LANGUAGES_FORM` stays unset until steps 4 and 5 of Required acceptance are done.

What it keeps from ADR-066: pages are built static and in English, the server reads no language, Corridra stores nothing about the choice, a language reaches the chip only through `CORRIDRA_LANGUAGES_REVIEWED`, and legal, consent and privacy text is not translated without a qualified reviewer. What it changes: on `/plan` only, the main region follows the chosen language when the form release names that language (ADR-066 "Page language and direction" keeps every main region English), and one new signal reaches the static host (see "What the static host can see"). That signal is an exception to ADR-066 ("What the server knows: Nothing"), which the owner accepted on 30 October 2026 for the request form's words files only. It still conflicts with the live privacy notice, which is changed before the switch is set (see Required acceptance).

## Context

ADR-066 releases a language through the chip and keeps every main region English. Since 25 October the home page's words follow the chosen language only for the languages named by `CORRIDRA_LANGUAGES_PAGES` (`apps/web/lib/i18n/pages.ts`); ADR-066 did not record that setting. The owner reports it is on in production; no production state was inspected for this record. On 29 October the owner authorised translating the public request form `/plan` behind an independent, off-by-default `CORRIDRA_LANGUAGES_FORM` that must not inherit the home-page release ([lane 11](../product/lane11-languages.md)). Legal, consent and privacy statements stay English, and the signed-in private request form, which mounts the same form component, stays English until its own phase. The first question inside the form keeps its own language behaviour, whatever the new setting says.

## Consequences

- The owner has one more switch, shown on the Owner page (`apps/web/lib/owner-switches.ts`). Its row shows which languages the setting names: Off, On, or the listed languages (for example "On for es, de"). The row does not read `CORRIDRA_LANGUAGES_REVIEWED`, so with `CORRIDRA_LANGUAGES_FORM=es,de` and only `es` reviewed it shows "On for es, de" while German visitors get English. The row's line tells the owner that a language must also be offered, which means it is named in `CORRIDRA_LANGUAGES_REVIEWED`, and that `CORRIDRA_LANGUAGES_PREVIEW` must stay unset on the public deployment.
- The form's words need their own row in the [language review record](../product/language-review-record.md), and that review is recorded before the switch is set.
- A later page (how it works, the signed-in screens) follows the same pattern: its own words module, its own release, its own review row.
- After load the form shows English for a moment while the chosen language's words arrive, as the chip already applies after load. Listen and the page language follow the region when it changes.
- Page length: at 1440 px wide the real form's last step is held to 1.08 times its English length (`tests/operator/ux-plan-languages.spec.ts`, the page-length budget). The reason is the English sentences on their own lines plus the line saying they are English, about 127 px in every language (design audit, 30 October 2026). Every other step keeps the design pass marks.
- Two page releases to reason about instead of one; the Owner page and the review record name each.
- Five rules in `apps/web/app/globals.css` now use logical sides (`border-inline-start`, `inset-inline-start` and `inset-inline-end`, `margin-inline-start`) so the draft notice, the journey bar line and the form's main button mirror in Arabic. In a left to right page they compute exactly as before. This is a further edit to the global file, after the chip move (ADR-066 item 4) and the Bengali font (ADR-066 question 1).
- The signed-in request page `/account/request/[workspaceId]` now also loads the English form words and the message reader with the form component. What it shows does not change.
- The static-host signal is a new fact about what leaves the browser. The owner accepted it on 30 October 2026. Until the privacy notice has been reviewed against it and changed after the owner's review, the switch stays unset. If the owner withdraws that acceptance, the per-language fetch is not released as built, and rejected option 5 is the fallback, which needs a further build.
- The promise that the form is translated only into reviewed languages rests on `CORRIDRA_LANGUAGES_PREVIEW` staying unset for the public, as ADR-066 already requires.

## Rejected options

1. Reuse `CORRIDRA_LANGUAGES_PAGES`. It is reported on in production, so unreviewed form words would go live, and the owner required independence.
2. Add the form words to the shared catalogue under a page prefix. Every page would download six languages of form words, and `localeFor` would tie the form to the home-page release.
3. Read the form release in the language provider. The signed-in private form renders the same component inside it and would be translated.
4. Render `/plan` per request with the language on the server. It gives up static pages (option C of the switcher proposal).
5. Put every released language in the page or its bundle. Every visitor of `/plan` would download every released language, most of them unused. Kept as the fallback if the owner withdraws the acceptance of the static-host signal above.

## Rollback

Unset `CORRIDRA_LANGUAGES_FORM` and redeploy: `/plan` is English and the same as the PR 214 form at every step (`tests/operator/ux-plan-languages.spec.ts` checks this with the setting off). A code rollback reverts the build commits. No data, migration, event, permission or money record is involved.

## Required acceptance

With `CORRIDRA_LANGUAGES_FORM` unset the request form is the PR 214 form. `tests/integration/language-form.test.ts`, `tests/browser/plan-language-words.spec.ts`, `tests/operator/ux-plan-languages.spec.ts`, `tests/operator/z-ux02-intake.spec.ts` and `tests/operator/real-use-intake.spec.ts` pass.

Before `CORRIDRA_LANGUAGES_FORM` is set to any value, the owner must, in this order:

1. Accept or reject the static-host signal described above. Done: the owner accepted it on 30 October 2026 ("accept signal, bn & others"). ADR-066 "What the server knows: Nothing" now has this one exception, the request form's words files, and holds as written for everything else.
2. Have the privacy-notice sentence on the language choice reviewed against that signal, and changed after the owner's review if it needs to change. The sentence today, in `apps/web/app/privacy/page.tsx`: "If you choose a language, your browser remembers the choice in local storage. It is not sent to us and you can clear it in your browser settings at any time." Legal and privacy text changes only after the owner's review ([UX charter](../product/ux-charter.md), section 4). Done in PR 216, merged to main as `b12e244` on 30 October 2026: the privacy notice shows the owner-approved sentence whenever the form release is on.
3. Record the form-words review for each language in the [review record](../product/language-review-record.md), with a qualified reviewer for each language. At that review the owner decides whether the consent lead instruction ("Read the notice and tick the box to continue.") counts as consent text. If it does, it either stays English like the rest of the consent wording, which needs a small change to the form, or it is released per language only once a qualified reviewer has approved it, as the owner chooses. Done: the owner approved the words of all six languages and the translated consent lead on 30 October 2026 ("approve all"). The owner, as decider, accepted his own review in place of a qualified reviewer for each language. No native-speaker check is recorded; a later native check can correct wording without holding back the release.
4. Name in `CORRIDRA_LANGUAGES_FORM` only languages that are also in `CORRIDRA_LANGUAGES_REVIEWED`, with `CORRIDRA_LANGUAGES_PREVIEW` unset on the public deployment. Still to do.
5. Set the value and redeploy. Both steps are the owner's. Still to do.

Steps 4 and 5 remain before the switch is set: `CORRIDRA_LANGUAGES_FORM` stays unset until both are done.
