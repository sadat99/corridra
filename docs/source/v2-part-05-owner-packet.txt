### 0. Mission

Complete the multi-corridor Corridra platform's operational controls, internal commercial visibility, administrative configuration, remaining technical readiness requirements and controlled launch mechanisms.

This part integrates and completes the former Parts 11–12 without replacing the successfully implemented Parts 01–08 or v2 Parts 1–4.

The intended operator is a small founder-led team managing multiple countries, missions, external partners and institutional customers. Design for clear decisions, low operating overhead and controlled scale.

Read accepted architecture/ADRs, every v2 release record, the original deferred-controls register and current system behaviour before implementation.

### 1. Global Corridor Control Center

Build a protected admin experience over the versioned configuration introduced in Part 1.

An authorized administrator should be able to:
- View configured countries, market scopes and routes.
- Create new corridor-route configuration from reusable templates.
- Enable/disable eligible mission types.
- Set supported languages, operating clusters and currencies.
- Configure approved providers/source adapters.
- Assign qualified operational owners/coverage.
- Manage qualification/requirement template versions.
- Manage approved AI task/model routes.
- Configure permitted communication mechanisms.
- Review active jurisdiction/privacy policy versions.
- Manage feature availability per route.
- Review capabilities and evidence required for route activation.
- Preview the effect of a proposed configuration change.
- Approve/publish/revert configuration versions where authorized.

No arbitrary execution of admin-entered code or unrestricted policy expressions.

No plaintext API-key retrieval.

No admin interface may weaken platform-wide authorization, retention or legal approval requirements.

New country-to-country routes should not require a database migration, new deployment or copy of another country's domain logic, provided their needed capabilities and adapters already exist.

### 2. Route and feature readiness

Each directional route must have its own operational readiness status.

Examples:
- BD→CN sourcing.
- BD→CN machinery.
- CN→BD market entry.
- BD→ES buyer access.
- ES→BD market entry.
- BD→EU broader market-access scope.

Readiness dimensions:
- Commercial offer and pricing approved.
- Named operational owner.
- Qualification capability.
- Actual available counterparty-research path.
- Approved communication methods.
- Language/operator coverage.
- Evidence requirements.
- Data-processing/retention/legal checks.
- External service dependencies.
- Customer support.
- Quality assurance and escalation.
- Deployment and rollback.
- Real-user pilot acceptance.

Display **PASS / BLOCKED / NOT APPLICABLE** with evidence and owner.

No route can become publicly advertised as fully operational merely because its configuration is technically valid.

Unknown or insufficient data should be shown as unknown, not zero or an extrapolated success claim.

### 6. Workflow reliability and observability

Preserve PostgreSQL transactional/outbox truth.

Implement or finish:
- Retry and backoff policy.
- Idempotent recovery.
- Dead-letter handling with accountable owner.
- Visible stuck task/communication/report queues.
- Operator-initiated safe recovery.
- Workflow version compatibility.
- Health and error monitoring.
- Privacy-safe request/mission correlation.
- Data-source/provider failures.
- Relevant infrastructure and AI cost visibility.

Use existing supported Vercel Workflow capabilities where appropriate; verify current provider constraints before extending.

Do not introduce Kubernetes, Kafka or a new queue product without a documented necessary reason.

Never log tokens, credentials, unrestricted personal data or confidential evidence.

### 7. Customer-data and infrastructure production gates

Audit and address every outstanding deferred-control item.

Specifically investigate and implement where technically and operationally possible:
- Private-repository main-branch release protection or documented equivalent.
- Secret scanning/push protection, including actual entitlement status.
- CodeQL/security scanning or a validated available alternative if entitlement blocks it.
- Secure production malware scanning and safe failure/quarantine.
- Appropriate EU production evidence storage and upload limits.
- Object lifecycle/orphan cleanup and hash reconciliation.
- Retention, lawful deletion, legal hold and DSAR handling.
- Privacy notices, controller/processor and data-transfer governance.
- Real customer-data environment separation.
- Real commercial outreach activation policy.
- Source-data licensing/rights.
- Backup/recovery/PITR checks.
- CSRF, SSRF, XSS/CSP, session security and rate-limit review.
- Independent release/permission regression testing.
- Previously deferred production commercial write acceptance.

Never silently close an item by changing its status text.

If a check requires legal counsel, a paid GitHub entitlement, an external provider account, actual operator approval or an unavailable credential, mark **BLOCKED: external prerequisite** and show the exact safe next action. Do not fabricate clearance.

Do not expose sensitive files or real customer records to an unreviewed testing environment.

### 8. Real-data activation design

Implement explicit environment separation for:
- Synthetic development/preview.
- Controlled production-candidate staging.
- Approved real-data production.

Do not reuse live production personal data for preview tests.

Real-data migration must preserve canonical history and provenance. Keep the existing synthetic environment intact until a separate production-data setup has passed readiness.

Production customer activation requires actual approval and recorded evidence for relevant jurisdiction, retention, storage, communications and operational controls.

Account signup being live does not automatically mean real Mission intake is permitted.

### 10. Release and expansion policy

Launch the two corridors asymmetrically:

**BD→China:** sourcing/machinery first, then delegations.

**BD→Spain/EU:** buyer access/export-market missions first, with reverse market-entry services selectively.

Other routes stay configurable but unpublished until ready.

Configure truthful route-specific product availability.

New-country release should require:
- Valid configuration.
- Available delivery capability.
- Named owner.
- Approved data/communications policies.
- Tested user journey.
- Commercial offer and support plan.
- Rollback.
- Explicit readiness approval.

Support ability to pause one route without disabling other routes or corrupting their data.
